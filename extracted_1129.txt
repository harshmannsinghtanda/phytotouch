Abstract
Question: 150 to 300 words, one paragraph. Cover the theme and SDG, the community problem, research 
findings, final problem statement, engineering knowledge, solution and prototype, key validation results, and 
impact. No references.
Solution
Post-harvest losses in citrus are a major problem in Indian mandis, where spoiled fruit is mixed with 
good fruit during manual grading. This reduces traders’ returns and wastes food, which relates to 
SDG 12, Target 12.3. Observations at [mandi name] and interviews with [number] [stakeholder 
groups] showed that grading is slow and inconsistent. Our problem statement is: small mandi 
traders need a low-cost, consistent way to grade citrus so that defective fruit is removed early. We 
developed CitrusSense, an autonomous optical sorter that uses in-browser AI vision to recognise 
citrus, grade it into A, B, C, D and Unripe categories, estimate shelf life, and show mandi value per 
fruit, with a conveyor mechatronics module. Validation used [N] lab-standard specimens and a 20-
photo benchmark, achieving [X]% grading accuracy at about [16] ms per scan. The prototype shows 
potential to reduce loss, but results depend on lighting and sample size, and [state what was tested 
on real hardware].
Keywords: citrus sorting, computer vision, post-harvest loss, mandi, shelf-life estimation, 
mechatronics

1.1 Introduction and Semester Theme
Question: Explain the semester theme in your own words and why it matters to society. State the objective of

matters because food lost after harvest reduces farmers’ income, wastes resources such as water 
and energy, and raises food prices for consumers. The objective of the Nirman Lab project is to 
identify a real post-harvest problem at a local market and build a working, low-cost engineering

Portfolio layer: Define | Nirman Lab journey: Week 5
3.1 Problem Analysis (5 Whys)
Question: Show the chain of Why questions from the observed problem to the root cause.
Solution
1. Why is citrus lost at the mandi? Spoiled fruit is mixed with good fruit.
2. Why is spoiled fruit mixed in? Grading is done quickly by eye and misses defects.
3. Why is grading missed? Workers are under time pressure and grade by experience.
4. Why is there no check? Small traders cannot afford automatic sorting equipment.
5. Why can they not afford it? No low-cost sorting tool exists for small mandis. (Root cause)

expensive), Measurement (no shelf-life check).
